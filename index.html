    How this works

    What every number on TradeMetric means and where it comes from.

    My Team and your lineup

    Your lineup is the highest-projected group of players that fits your league's roster slots. If you imported from Sleeper or ESPN, it shows the lineup you actually set there.

    Each projection starts from his points per game this season, blended with preseason expectations early in the year. It is then adjusted for this week's opponent, his team's expected points from the betting line, his injury status and the factors listed under Player values.

    Roster strength compares your starters at each position with the league average. Weakest spot is the position furthest below it.

    Start or sit

    By default it compares the closest call in your lineup: the starter whose projection is nearest to a bench player who could replace him. You can pick any two or three of your players, or type in anyone.

    Floor and ceiling come from how much his scoring has swung week to week. Clear call means a gap of 3 or more points, Lean 1 to 3, and Coin flip under 1.

    Player values, experts and risk

    A player's value is the points he is projected to score from here on, compared with the best player you could get off waivers at his position. Current production counts most.

    This week's projection is 90% ESPN's and Sleeper's own projections, averaged and scored with your league's settings, and 10% TradeMetric's model. The platforms update theirs through the week as injury news and depth charts change, so the site checks them every 5 minutes. If both platforms leave a player out, they're projecting him for zero, usually because he's out. Each player's card shows all three numbers.

    The Experts rank is the FantasyPros consensus for the rest of the season. It makes up 40% of rest-of-season values, so a proven player off to a slow start is not written off after one bad month.

    Risk (Low, Medium, High) adds up injury status, games missed, how much the experts disagree, big week-to-week swings and small samples. Risk lowers a player's value in trades a little; it never removes him.

    What moves his projection lists small adjustments, each a few percent and capped at 8% in total:

    • Rest of season: strength of defenses faced so far, targets and air yards compared with his scoring, running back age and heavy workloads, touchdown luck, a rising or falling snap share, and a rising or falling workload.
    • This week: game script from the betting line, indoor or cold games, a backup quarterback starting, a first game back from a soft-tissue injury, a short week, coming off a bye, West Coast teams in early road games, injured offensive linemen, and injured defensive backs on the other team.
    Rankings and tags

    Players are ranked by value over a waiver-level player for your league's scoring and roster. Pts/G is projected points per game from here on.

    BL Buy low: a good player who scored less than expected lately but is still getting the ball, or whom experts rank well above how he has scored. SH Sell high: scoring above what his role or the experts support. RISK: scoring above his workload, or facing a hard schedule. Defenses and kickers never get buy or sell tags.

    Trend is his last three games against his season average. Opp/G is carries plus targets per game.

    Waiver wire and bids

    Your gain is how many points a week your best lineup changes after adding him and dropping the player shown. Pts/G ahead blends TradeMetric's projection with the experts' rest-of-season ranks.

    The suggested bid is a share of your season budget. It rises with his value to you and to the league, rises when other teams need his position and when he is moving into a bigger role, and falls as fewer weeks are left. In leagues that use waiver order, it suggests whether he is worth a high claim instead.

    Trade values

    Each player gets a 0 to 100 score: half from trade market values (FantasyCalc, KeepTradeCut and FantasyPros) and half from TradeMetric's rest-of-season projection for your scoring. Stars count for more than the sum of lesser players, so two 30s rarely get you a 60.

    Tiers group players with similar scores at each position. In keeper and dynasty leagues, long-term value is blended in.

    Trade suggestions and grades

    TradeMetric looks at every deal from one-for-one up to three-for-two with each team, then keeps the ones that help you and that the other manager has a reason to accept: fair market value, a better lineup for them, or filling a need.

    The shape fits each team's situation. A team with depth to spare gets offered a consolidation, two players for one better starter. A team with injured starters or holes in its lineup gets offered extra bodies. Trades that would force a team to cut a starter are left out.

    Your grade weighs points added each week, your playoff weeks (15 to 17 count extra), and the change in your playoff and title odds, minus injury and stacking risk. What counts most depends on where you stand: contenders care about the playoffs, teams on the bubble about winning now.

    Realistic trades. Every deal is also checked the way a real manager would see it:

    • Starting lineup, before and after: each team's best starting lineup in points per week over the rest of the season, now and after the trade. Suggestions only show deals where both lineups get better or stay about even.
    • True VAR: a player's value is his points per game above a replacement-level starter at his position, raised to the 1.2 power, so one elite player is worth more than two lesser ones with the same total.
    • Drop cost: in a 2-for-1 or bigger, the team that ends up over the roster limit has to cut its least valuable bench player. That player's VAR counts against the deal.
    • Consolidation tax: giving a top-5 player at his position for a package of lesser players is penalized unless the starting lineup gets at least 3 points a week better. That's why other managers rarely accept "two dimes for a quarter."
    • Team profiles: each team is a Win-Now Contender, on the Playoff Bubble, or Out of the Race (from the Season Sim), and Depth-Rich / Stud-Needy or Star-Heavy / Zero-Depth (from its roster). Contenders care most about Weeks 15–17, bubble teams about the next few weeks. Deals that turn one team's surplus into the other's need score higher.
    • Realism Score (0–100): combines both lineup changes, how close the market values are, the other team's reasons to say yes and whether the deal fits both profiles, minus drop costs, consolidation tax and oversized packages. 80+ is very realistic, 62+ realistic, 45+ a stretch, below that unlikely.
    Season Sim

    Weeks already played use real results. The rest of the season is simulated 2,000 times from every team's projected lineup, byes and matchups. Ties in the standings go to points scored.

    What needs to happen groups the simulated seasons by your final record and shows how often each record makes the playoffs.

    Matchups and schedule

    Defense ranks show how many fantasy points each defense gives up to a position this season. 1st gives up the fewest (hardest matchup) and 32nd the most (easiest). Green means easy, red means tough. Weeks 15 to 17 are the fantasy playoffs.

    Team total is the points his team is expected to score, from the betting line.

    News and practice

    Stories come from ESPN, CBS, Yahoo, ProFootballTalk, RotoBaller, Pro Football Network, The Athletic and Google News. The same story from different outlets is combined, and the four most useful recent ones are shown. Titles and summaries are written by AI from each story.

    Practice reports show his final practice of each week from the official NFL injury report.

    Draft tools

    Draft values use value over a replacement-level starter for your league's scoring and roster. During the season they are rest-of-season values; next summer they switch to full-season projections.

    Mock draft: computer drafters pick by value and roster need, with some randomness. Live draft: keep it open during your real draft and tap each pick as it happens. Best picks favor open starting spots and warn when a player probably won't last until your next pick.

    League type, waivers and QB value

    Keeper and dynasty leagues blend in long-term value (age, draft year and DynastyProcess market values) for trades, trade values and the draft. Redraft leagues count this season only.

    Waiver budget sets the dollar amounts for suggested bids.

    Quarterback value depends on league size. In one-QB leagues quarterbacks are easy to replace, more so in small leagues, so their trade value is scaled down: about a third in 8-team leagues, half in 12-team leagues and nearly full value in very large ones. A standout still keeps a big edge because he beats the best waiver QB by a lot. Superflex and 2QB leagues use full value.

    Alerts

    The bell flags starters who are out, doubtful, questionable or on bye, sell-high windows on your players, and big pickups when a starter gets hurt. Alerts show inside the site.

    Where the data comes from

    Stats, schedules, betting lines, injury reports, depth charts and snap counts come from nflverse. Expert rankings (weekly and rest of season), roster rates and dynasty values come from FantasyPros via DynastyProcess. Market values come from FantasyCalc and KeepTradeCut. Every 5 minutes the site also pulls live: ESPN and Sleeper weekly projections, injury designations and ESPN injury notes (including practice participation), betting lines (DraftKings, via ESPN) until kickoff, FantasyCalc trade values and player news. During games, scores and stats update about every 2 minutes. Season stats, snap counts, expert rankings and KeepTradeCut values refresh each morning and again after each game day.
